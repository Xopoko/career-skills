# Career Direction

Turn uncertainty into a small set of role hypotheses and reversible experiments.
Do not present a personality label as destiny.

Read `../../references/career-direction-and-development.md` and the evidence
rules in `../../references/evidence-contract.md`.

## Procedure

1. Frame the decision, horizon, urgency, and acceptable risk.
2. Load confirmed preferences, constraints, goals, and most relevant evidence.
3. Ask for missing information only when it changes the next experiment.
4. Generate three to five role-family hypotheses, including an adjacent option
   and a conservative bridge when useful.
5. For each, map recurring work, environment, transferable evidence, real gaps,
   proof gaps, constraints, market unknowns, and likely tradeoffs.
6. If occupational reference data helps, use a current, versioned O*NET or ESCO
   source and preserve mapping strength. Do not infer possession of a skill from
   an occupation record.
7. Compare hypotheses with visible criteria and hard gates. Keep unknowns
   visible; do not manufacture a single authoritative score.
8. Design the cheapest reversible experiment for the top hypotheses.
9. Define a decision date, evidence to collect, and stop or pivot condition.
10. Record user-confirmed direction and preferences through `career-context`.

## Pivot Narrative

Translate evidence at the level of problems, decisions, mechanisms, and results.
State:

- what the user has repeatedly done;
- why it transfers;
- what context is new;
- how the user is testing or closing that gap;
- why the target work fits their stated constraints and motivation.

Do not upgrade exposure to ownership, change titles, or hide a material gap.

## Output

Provide:

- decision frame and hard constraints;
- comparable role-family table;
- evidence already portable to each path;
- gaps split into knowledge, practice, proof, signal, credential, eligibility,
  and preference;
- ranked reversible experiments;
- the next decision and its evidence threshold.

Route current demand or pay questions to `career-market-research`, active
openings to `opportunity-search`, and a growth plan to `career-development`.
