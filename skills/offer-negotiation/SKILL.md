# Offer Negotiation

Normalize written terms, clarify uncertainty, and help the user decide. Do not
accept, decline, or send a counter without a separate approved effect.

Read:

- `../../references/offer-and-negotiation.md`
- `../../references/market-and-opportunity-research.md`
- `../../references/safety-privacy-fairness.md`

## Procedure

1. Obtain the current written offer and authoritative deadline.
2. Confirm jurisdiction, employment basis, location, currency, and decision
   context. Route legal, tax, investment, or immigration interpretation to
   current qualified sources.
3. Extract base, variable pay, equity, benefits, working conditions, role scope,
   start, trial, notice, termination, restrictions, repayment, and unknowns.
4. Preserve raw terms, then normalize with visible formulas, exchange-rate date,
   hours, vesting assumptions, and unpriced items.
5. Load the user's must-haves, dealbreakers, priorities, alternatives, and risk
   tolerance. Keep walk-away and private fallback values private.
6. Research current comparable evidence when it could change the decision.
7. Compare financial and nonfinancial dimensions with explicit weights and
   uncertainty.
8. Ask whether the user wants to assess negotiation. Never assume every offer
   should be negotiated.
9. If yes, identify negotiable terms, decision authority, grounded rationale,
   prioritized ask, and package alternatives.
10. Draft the counter or clarification message and review relationship risk,
    channel, timing, and deadline.
11. Create a communication effect plan and stop before sending.
12. Verify any revision in writing before updating the decision or closing the
    pipeline.

## Output

Return normalized terms, formulas, unknowns, risks, weighted tradeoffs,
market-evidence limits, recommendation options, negotiation plan, private
fallback section, outbound draft, and effect boundary. Do not promise a gain or
assign speculative equity a guaranteed value.
