# Interview Preparation

Prepare for the actual stage and format using verified evidence. Teach response
structure before repeated practice.

Read:

- `../../references/interview-playbook.md`
- `../../references/evidence-contract.md`
- `../../references/safety-privacy-fairness.md`
- `../../templates/story-card.example.md`

## Procedure

1. Confirm opportunity, stage, format, participants, duration, timezone,
   assessment rules, and preparation deadline.
2. Refresh the posting and material employer or role facts.
3. Derive a competency map from explicit responsibilities and stage; label
   inferred themes.
4. Retrieve active facts and select a small diverse story set. Do not create a
   story from resume prose alone.
5. Build past-behavior and situational questions plus role-specific work-sample
   themes when appropriate.
6. Teach the response structure: direct answer, minimum context, responsibility,
   actions and reasoning, observed result, reflection or relevance.
7. Run one question at a time. Let the user finish unless interruption was
   requested.
8. Assess against declared role-relevant anchors, cite what worked, and propose
   one or two changes.
9. Retry the affected section, then the whole answer when useful.
10. Prepare interviewer questions that resolve real decision unknowns.
11. Review references, logistics, accessibility, confidentiality, and assessment
   integrity.
    If an accessibility or tool barrier appears, route to a minimum-disclosure
    accommodation request; do not ask for diagnosis details or reduce fit.
12. After the interview, capture observations, new claims, exact next step,
   proposed or confirmed timing, and a follow-up draft.

## Domain Assessments

Use an appropriate domain skill for technical or craft accuracy when one is
available. Career owns role mapping, evidence selection, truthful response
structure, logistics, and debrief. Never bypass assessment rules or
misrepresent authorship.

## Output

Provide the competency map, selected stories and evidence IDs, practice rubric,
targeted feedback, interviewer questions, logistics checklist, and debrief
template. A practice score is not a hiring forecast.
