# Career Direction And Development

Career direction is a sequence of testable choices, not a personality verdict.
The output should narrow the next experiment while preserving options.

## Direction Frame

Capture only decision-relevant inputs:

- desired work outcomes and problems worth solving;
- energizing and draining activities, with examples rather than labels;
- preferred craft, product, people, operational, or commercial balance;
- autonomy, pace, ambiguity, collaboration, visibility, and leadership needs;
- location, remote mode, schedule, travel, language, authorization, income,
  contract, health, caregiving, and timing constraints;
- existing evidence, transferable capabilities, missing proof, and learning
  appetite;
- acceptable risk, runway, and reversibility.

Interests and values may guide exploration, but they do not establish aptitude,
eligibility, or fit. Do not infer a protected trait or health status.

## Role-Family Comparison

Compare three to five role families using the same frame:

1. Core outcomes and recurring work.
2. Typical environments and decision scope.
3. Transferable evidence already available.
4. Important gaps split into knowledge, practice, signal, and credential.
5. Hard constraints and likely tradeoffs.
6. Current market evidence for the user's geography and work mode.
7. Smallest reversible experiment.

Use O*NET or ESCO as versioned occupational reference data when useful. Store
taxonomy identifiers, release, language, and mapping strength. Taxonomy averages
are priors, not a vacancy's requirements or proof of a user's capability.

## Transferable Evidence

Translate at the level of outcomes and mechanisms:

- problem and stakeholders;
- decisions owned;
- constraints handled;
- methods and tools used;
- observed result;
- reusable capability;
- target-role terminology;
- remaining context gap.

Do not rename a responsibility into a more senior one or turn exposure into
ownership. Mark terminology translation as a derivation linked to source facts.

## Experiment Portfolio

Prefer several bounded probes over one irreversible leap. Possible probes:

- two informational conversations with people doing the target work;
- one small portfolio artifact that demonstrates the missing mechanism;
- one realistic task simulation with a rubric;
- one targeted application batch after evidence repair;
- a temporary internal project, shadowing request, or mentoring session;
- a course module only when it unlocks a specific performance task;
- a compensation or demand research sprint in the actual geography.

Each experiment has a question, cost ceiling, timebox, observable result, stop
condition, and decision date. Completion alone is not success; record what was
learned and whether the role hypothesis changed.

## Gap Classification

- `knowledge`: concepts can be learned and checked.
- `practice`: skill needs realistic repetitions and feedback.
- `proof`: capability exists but lacks a defensible artifact or story.
- `signal`: proof exists but is not discoverable or legible to the target
  audience.
- `credential`: a current source confirms a required formal qualification.
- `eligibility`: location, authorization, license, or schedule must be checked.
- `preference`: the user may not want the recurring work or tradeoff.

Do not prescribe education merely because a posting lists it as preferred.
First inspect substitution evidence, current requirements, cost, completion
risk, and a cheaper proof path.

## Development Plan

Create a 30/60/90-day or milestone plan only after connecting it to a target
role and repeated evidence gap. Include:

- capability outcome, not merely course completion;
- baseline evidence;
- practice task and feedback source;
- proof artifact and audience;
- weekly effort ceiling and recovery rule;
- checkpoint evidence and abandon/adjust criteria;
- budget and external effect gates;
- next role or pipeline decision the work should unlock.

Balance capability, visibility, relationships, and opportunity exposure.
Progress can include a clearer rejection of a poor-fit direction.
