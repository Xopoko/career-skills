# Interview Preparation And Debrief

Preparation starts from the actual role, stage, format, and evidence. It should
improve truthful structure, not manufacture a persona.

## Stage Map

- recruiter or initial screen: motivation, constraints, headline evidence,
  process, and basic alignment;
- hiring lead: outcomes, scope, judgment, collaboration, and role problem;
- behavioral panel: competencies, past behavior, reflection, and consistency;
- technical or craft assessment: task reasoning, tradeoffs, communication,
  verification, and recovery from uncertainty;
- case or presentation: problem framing, assumptions, analysis, recommendation,
  limits, and questions;
- leadership or values discussion: decisions, systems, conflict, development,
  ethics, and organizational impact;
- final or offer-stage conversation: mutual fit, unresolved risk, level, scope,
  expectations, and next steps.

## Competency Map

Derive likely competencies from explicit responsibilities and stage. For each,
record the evidence anchor and whether the prompt should test past behavior,
situational judgment, knowledge, or work sample. Keep inferred themes labeled.

Avoid charisma, accent, appearance, protected traits, and vague culture-fit
scoring. Use role-relevant anchors such as ownership, action, reasoning,
tradeoffs, collaboration, result, and reflection.

## Story Bank

Use `templates/story-card.example.md`. Build stories only from active facts and
user-confirmed context. Select a small diverse set covering success, setback,
conflict, ambiguity, leadership, craft, learning, and ethics as relevant.

Teach a concise response structure before repetitive practice:

1. answer the question directly;
2. give the minimum context and stakes;
3. state personal responsibility;
4. explain key actions and reasoning;
5. give the observed result;
6. reflect or connect to the target role.

Numbers are optional and must be supported. Qualitative outcomes can be strong
when concrete.

## Practice Loop

1. Ask one realistic question.
2. Let the user answer without interruption unless requested.
3. Assess against declared anchors.
4. Cite what worked from the answer.
5. Identify one or two high-leverage changes.
6. Retry the affected section, then the full answer if useful.
7. Record no new fact until the user confirms it.

Separate communication coaching from truth review. Do not claim a practice
rating predicts hiring.

## Role-Specific Assessment

For technical, design, data, product, sales, operations, research, or leadership
work, inspect the actual task type and use the appropriate domain skill when
available. Career owns evidence selection, stage strategy, response structure,
and debrief; it does not invent domain expertise.

For take-home work, clarify allowed tools, timebox, intellectual-property risk,
evaluation criteria, and whether the task resembles uncompensated production
work. Never misrepresent authorship or bypass assessment rules.

## Questions For The Interviewer

Prioritize questions that resolve a real decision:

- success in 30, 90, and 180 days;
- role origin and current problem;
- decision authority and interfaces;
- team constraints and operating rhythm;
- quality and feedback mechanisms;
- level expectations and growth;
- process, timeline, and unresolved terms.

Avoid questions already answered by a current official source unless checking
an inconsistency.

## Debrief

Soon after the interaction, separate:

- questions asked and observed responses;
- user answers and evidence used;
- new employer or role claims needing verification;
- moments of uncertainty or missing proof;
- stage and next step stated by the interviewer;
- proposed versus confirmed timing;
- follow-up draft and approval boundary;
- pipeline event and development feedback.

Do not rewrite a difficult interaction as failure before an external outcome is
known.
