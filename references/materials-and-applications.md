# Career Materials And Applications

Materials are audience-specific views over the same career facts. Maintain one
truth layer and many versioned artifacts.

## Baseline Asset Types

- chronological, functional-hybrid, technical, leadership, academic, or
  creative resume as justified by the target;
- professional profile and short biography;
- accomplishment inventory and story bank;
- portfolio index and evidence-backed case studies;
- project sheet, publication list, speaking list, or credential index;
- reference-permission and briefing sheet;
- reusable application-data sheet with sensitivity labels.

Do not produce every variant by default. Start with the artifact that unlocks
the next decision or application.

## Resume Contract

1. Define audience, geography, role family, language, page constraint, and file
   format.
2. Select supported evidence before drafting.
3. Order sections by relevance and defensibility.
4. Write concise action, scope, reasoning, and result where supported.
5. Preserve qualitative impact when no verified metric exists.
6. Keep dates, titles, employers, credentials, and links consistent with active
   facts.
7. Avoid tables, graphics, headers, or parsing-sensitive layouts when the
   destination requires a simple machine-readable resume.
8. Render and inspect the final document when output format matters.
9. For PDF, independently extract and inspect the machine-readable text layer.
   Request UTF-8 output where supported, then check reading order, literal
   contact fields and dates, missing text, and replacement glyphs. If extraction
   is unavailable, record degraded QA rather than a pass.
10. Build and validate a claim manifest.

Do not optimize to an invented ATS score, force keyword density, or add a skill
because the posting mentions it.

## Portfolio Case Study

Separate public-safe evidence from confidential detail:

- context and problem;
- role, responsibility, and collaborators;
- constraints and alternatives;
- actions and reasoning;
- artifact or mechanism demonstrated;
- observed result and source;
- reflection and changed practice;
- what is redacted or generalized;
- fact and evidence identifiers.

Never expose private code, data, client identity, security detail, or employer
material without permission.

## Role-Specific Tailoring

1. Validate the opportunity and current posting.
2. Load hard constraints and the evidence matrix.
3. Choose a defensible candidate angle.
4. Select facts that prove the most important outcomes.
5. Change ordering and terminology without changing meaning.
6. Address material gaps honestly or leave them for clarification.
7. Draft only the requested artifacts.
8. Run a reviewer pass for truth, specificity, user voice, role relevance,
   internal consistency, formatting, and unsupported claims.
9. Generate artifact receipt and claim manifest.

## Cover Letters And Short Answers

A useful letter or answer connects one real employer or role need to a small
number of supported examples. It should add reasoning or motivation that a
resume cannot show. Avoid generic praise, copied mission language, and invented
personal attachment.

For a required question:

- answer the exact question and word limit;
- distinguish eligibility or legal fields from narrative fields;
- cite supported facts in the manifest;
- surface an unknown instead of filling it with a likely answer;
- retain the final submitted version and opportunity link in the artifact index.

## Review Roles

Use a separate reviewer pass after drafting:

- `truth reviewer`: claims and dates;
- `role reviewer`: evidence-to-criterion coverage;
- `voice reviewer`: natural language and user ownership;
- `risk reviewer`: sensitive data, confidentiality, and overclaiming;
- `render reviewer`: layout, links, truncation, and file integrity.

Review findings do not become facts. Revise, regenerate the claim manifest, and
re-run validation.
