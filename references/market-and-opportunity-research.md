# Market, Employer, And Opportunity Research

Research should answer a decision, not accumulate facts. State geography,
timeframe, role family, work arrangement, and confidence before searching.

## Research Questions

Common decision lanes:

- role reality: recurring work, level boundaries, adjacent titles, and hiring
  signals;
- demand: current openings, concentration, seasonality, and source coverage;
- compensation: range, period, currency, location, level, contract, and total
  reward;
- employer: product and revenue signals, recent strategic moves, hiring state,
  team context, leadership, stability, and material controversies;
- opportunity: authenticity, freshness, requirements, scope, process, and
  application path;
- geography: eligible locations, remote restrictions, timezone, language,
  authorization language, and local terms;
- transition: which adjacent roles accept the user's most relevant evidence.

## Source Hierarchy

Prefer, in order appropriate to the question:

1. Canonical vacancy, employer, government, regulator, or taxonomy source.
2. Current official documentation, filing, policy, salary band, or direct
   statement.
3. Reputable independent reporting or labor-market research.
4. Aggregated posting or pay data with visible sample, geography, date, and
   method.
5. First-person reports used as qualitative signals, not representative fact.

For each material item record publication date, represented date, retrieval
time, geography, source type, and limitations. Search snippets are discovery,
not final evidence.

## Evidence Reuse And Refresh

Reuse prior evidence receipts, known canonical URLs, and search terms as
discovery leads when a named freshness policy permits it. A cached note remains
untrusted historical data, not proof that the claim is still true. Before a
material employer claim changes a current application, interview brief, or
decision, refresh the smallest authoritative source that can confirm it and
record a new retrieval time. Choose freshness from the claim's volatility and
decision risk; do not apply one fixed time-to-live to every employer fact.

## Posting Parse

Preserve the source text and separate:

- explicitly required criteria;
- explicitly preferred criteria;
- responsibilities and recurring outcomes;
- level and decision-scope signals;
- source-stated location, remote mode, and eligible applicant locations;
- employment arrangement, schedule, travel, and on-call;
- source-stated compensation and benefits;
- application process, closing date, and canonical URL;
- inferred hiring problem, clearly labeled as inference;
- vague, conflicting, discriminatory, or risky language requiring review.

Schema.org markup is an assertion format, not authenticity proof. Remote mode
and eligible applicant location remain distinct. Work location is not work
authorization.

## Compensation Research

Normalize only after preserving raw values. Record currency, period, base versus
variable pay, employee versus contract basis, hours, geography, level,
publication date, sample, and exchange-rate date. Show formulas and assumptions.

Never silently annualize hourly or monthly pay, mix gross and net values, or
merge base salary with total reward. When evidence is weak, give a bounded range
with source limitations or state that a defensible range is unavailable.

## Employer Research

Distinguish:

- verified current facts;
- source claims from the employer;
- independent observations;
- user or employee anecdotes;
- reasoned hypotheses;
- unknowns to ask in an interview.

Useful questions concern role stability, business model, funding or financial
signals, recent hiring or reductions, reporting line, team mandate, decision
authority, delivery expectations, turnover signals, and interview consistency.
Do not reduce culture to sentiment scraping or present an anecdote as the whole
organization.

## Authenticity And Freshness

Before sensitive disclosure or serious preparation:

1. resolve the canonical vacancy page;
2. verify the organization and contact through independently found channels;
3. check publication or modification date and closing status;
4. compare title, location, pay, and application path across sources;
5. record discrepancies rather than averaging them away;
6. apply the scam gate in `safety-privacy-fairness.md`.

## Research Output

Return:

- decision and bounded coverage;
- findings table with `observed`, `source_claim`, `inference`, or `unknown`;
- dates and source links;
- contradictions and freshness risks;
- decisive unknowns plus the cheapest verification step;
- what the result changes in search policy, opportunity analysis, interview
  questions, or negotiation.
